# Sail An Average Campaign Into Bullet-Hell Waters

Build the game on An Average Campaign's voted, party-based roguelite skeleton, then add three systems it does not have. Keep what AAC has proven on Roblox: a social tavern lobby, crews of 1–5 players (3–4 is the sweet spot), runs of fights broken up by group-voted encounter cards, subclass mentors tied to specific areas, gear that resets each run, and a light meta currency. Then make three changes. First, replace AAC's dice-rolled Block/Dodge with a per-player, Undertale-style soul box that runs during every enemy turn. AAC has no bullet hell at all, so this is your own differentiator, and it answers the game's loudest public complaint: that RNG combat "takes no skill." Second, add Darkest Dungeon's ranks, stress meter and Death's Door as the attrition layer, tuned so an affliction changes numbers instead of taking a human player's character away from them. Third, put AAC's "pick one of three" encounter screen inside an Inscryption-style sea chart on the ship's table. Lay the chart out with Slay the Spire's placement rules and hide the far side with fog, the way a compass that only points to the next island would. The truly complicated puzzles go in a persistent ship hub, so a failed voyage still moves something forward. One Piece's world maps onto these systems almost one-to-one: a compass that points only to the next island is a node map, Devil Fruit types are class archetypes, the three kinds of Haki are attack, defense and dodge layers, crew jobs are Darkest Dungeon roles, and bounties are a heat stat. The niche is also empty, because every major One Piece-inspired Roblox game is a real-time grinder or a PvP brawler. The hard constraint is IP. One Piece cannot be licensed on Roblox today, its rights holders already run official games there, and Roblox gives them fast removal tools. So the game should ship an original world with renamed terms and keep "One Piece" out of its title.

*How to read this report: a sentence with an inline citation is a sourced fact from the research notes. Paragraphs and table columns labelled **Design call** are recommendations built from those facts. None of them has been tested on Roblox, and their numbers are starting values for prototyping, not measurements.*

## An Average Campaign is a voted, dice-rolled D&D roguelite with no bullet hell

The functional reference is a mid-to-large Roblox hit made by a very small team. An Average Campaign (universe 7359962123) is owned by a group with four credited members: huhuzh (scripting and UI), two modelers, and one animator/VFX artist. Fan sites call the team Regicide Studio. On November 14, 2026 the live build was **Alpha v0.5.2 "Cauldro's Return"** ([Roblox game page](https://www.roblox.com/games/80734098185936/An-Average-Campaign); [Miraheze Version History](https://anaveragecampaign.miraheze.org/wiki/Version_History)). On that date it had about **64.4M visits, 288.5K favorites and 94.4% approval from roughly 99.8K votes** ([Roblox Games API](https://games.roblox.com/v1/games?universeIds=7359962123); [Rotrends](https://www.rotrends.com/game/7359962123/An-Average-Campaign-Alpha-v051)). It peaked at **9,633 concurrent players on May 31, 2026**, was drawing about 2,000–3,700 concurrent players a day in November, and has an **average playtime of 26.06 minutes** ([Rolimons](https://www.rolimons.com/game/80734098185936)). The official description calls it a "classic, simplistic roguelike" for **3–4 players that supports 1–5**, names **Dungeons & Dragons, Arcane Lineage and Slay the Spire** as inspirations, and warns that it "is intended to be difficult" ([Roblox game page](https://www.roblox.com/games/80734098185936/An-Average-Campaign)). Lobby servers hold 50 players, private servers are disabled, and every avatar is forced into a blocky R6 rig ([Roblox Games API](https://games.roblox.com/v1/games?universeIds=7359962123)).

The loop is a Slay the Spire-style run dressed in tabletop trappings. In a tavern lobby, players pick a class and race at a Magic Mirror, buy Boons from a Tavern Master, and form a party at an NPC called the Aura Farmer ([an-average-campaign.pages.dev](https://an-average-campaign.pages.dev/)). A run is a sequence of fights. After each fight the party picks one of **three weighted-random encounters rated with difficulty stars**, or chooses Scavenge or a Short Rest instead ([Miraheze Encounters](https://anaveragecampaign.miraheze.org/wiki/Encounters)). The area order is fixed: **Forest (8 stages, boss: the Giant), then Dungeon (12 stages, boss: Bone Drake)**. Two branches are optional. The **Dwarven Mines (7 stages)** open through an Elven Camp encounter in rounds 4–7, and the **Sewer (5 stages)** opens through an Ominous Manhole in rounds 3–8 ([Miraheze Areas](https://anaveragecampaign.miraheze.org/wiki/Areas)). Class, items and Boons make specific encounters more likely. An Adventurer's Compass, for example, raises both branch gateways to weight 30, so choosing a route is part of choosing a build ([Miraheze Encounters](https://anaveragecampaign.miraheze.org/wiki/Encounters)). A standard clear is about 20 fights, which explains why the studio added mid-run rejoining in v0.4.2.1 ([Miraheze Version History](https://anaveragecampaign.miraheze.org/wiki/Version_History)). The run ends only when the whole party falls ([an-average-campaign.pages.dev](https://an-average-campaign.pages.dev/)). A separate permanent "Timestream Entanglement" one-shot mode has its own linear route. Its true ending requires killing every optional boss in a single run ([Miraheze Timestream Entanglement](https://anaveragecampaign.miraheze.org/wiki/Timestream_Entanglement)).

Combat is **fully turn-based, with no real-time input of any kind**, and this corrects the premise in the brief. Turn order comes from an Initiative roll. On each turn a character picks **Fight, Items or Focus** (Focus banks Energy) and "cannot flee or skip turns." Abilities cost Energy and have cooldowns: Lunge, for example, costs 2 Energy with a 6-turn cooldown ([an-average-campaign.pages.dev](https://an-average-campaign.pages.dev/); [Miraheze Equipment](https://anaveragecampaign.miraheze.org/wiki/Equipment)). Defense is passive dice: "**Each hit rolls Block and Dodge separately**." DEX adds 0.175% Dodge per point, and Dodge caps at 80% ([an-average-campaign.pages.dev](https://an-average-campaign.pages.dev/); [Miraheze Status Effects](https://anaveragecampaign.miraheze.org/wiki/Status_Effects)). Enemies bank Energy and use heavier moves when they have more of it. The Giant double-strikes at 2 Energy and sweeps at high Energy, and that buildup is the game's only telegraph ([an-average-campaign.pages.dev](https://an-average-campaign.pages.dev/)). No wiki, guide or inspected gameplay frame shows a soul box, projectile arena, QTE or parry ([Wetlag full-run video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=e83JrLu2YZA&t=1620s); [Miraheze Status Effects](https://anaveragecampaign.miraheze.org/wiki/Status_Effects)). One fan defending the game put it plainly: "It's supposed to be more similar to D&D which is why it's rng based" ([Eclipse review comments](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VYNmn4Tp1Jo)). The bullet-hell layer is therefore this project's own addition and should be designed from Undertale and Deltarune, not copied from AAC. A plausible source of the mix-up is an unrelated satire game, *Average Roblox Undertale Game* ([RoWatcher](https://rowatcher.com/games/3346730273/balance-overhaul-average-roblox-undertale-game)). Researchers could not completely rule out a timed-defense feature hidden in the October 2026 patch, but nothing suggests one exists.

Builds take shape during the run. Seven base classes each pair two stats (Warrior STR+CON, Bard CHA+INT, and so on). They branch into **18–19 subclasses, each unlocked by meeting a trainer encounter in a specific area**. A Warrior, for example, becomes a Rampager in the Forest, a Knight in the Dungeon, or a Grudge Settler in the Mines, and "subclasses last the entire run" ([an-average-campaign.pages.dev](https://an-average-campaign.pages.dev/)). Each level gives +3 stat points and +2 max HP up to about level 25, and crafted gear "lasts only for the current run" ([an-average-campaign.pages.dev](https://an-average-campaign.pages.dev/)). What carries over between runs is options, not power you keep. The **Cealt** currency (about 70 per full clear) buys Boons for **5 slots**, Races, and class **Prestige** (up to 25 levels at 3 skill points each). **Game Modifiers** trade difficulty for Cealt multipliers: Towering Forces, for example, sets enemy HP to ×1.35 for +20% Cealts ([Miraheze Customization](https://anaveragecampaign.miraheze.org/wiki/Customization); [Miraheze Game Modifiers](https://anaveragecampaign.miraheze.org/wiki/Game_Modifiers)). A fallen player can be revived at a Dungeon Altar for 250 gold, by a Travelling Necromancer for 100 gold, or with a **50-Robux "Revive in a run"** product ([an-average-campaign.pages.dev](https://an-average-campaign.pages.dev/); [Roblox Developer Products API](https://apis.roblox.com/developer-products/v2/universes/7359962123/developerproducts?limit=50)). Monetization is light: **no gamepasses and 18 developer products**, most of them Cealt bundles priced from 30 to 500 Robux ([Rotrends](https://www.rotrends.com/game/7359962123/An-Average-Campaign-Alpha-v051)).

Between fights, the "D&D layer" is narrated events with odds based on stats. The official Trello lists formulas for them. The Potion Seller needs a teammate with 20+ INT, succeeds at 20% + INT×4%, and on failure can kill outright. The Busted Caravan succeeds at 10% + STR×3.5%, and on failure starts a goblin fight ([Official Trello JSON](https://trello.com/b/12spe143.json)). AAC has **no puzzles and no stress, sanity or morale meter**. Morale-like effects exist only as combat statuses such as Rallied and Demoralized ([Miraheze Status Effects](https://anaveragecampaign.miraheze.org/wiki/Status_Effects)). The sharpest public critique is YouTuber Eclipse's 112K-view video. It targets **party matchmaking friction, RNG-heavy combat, and a trial-and-error loop** that rewards knowing which NPC choice pays out, and it says turn-based combat "takes no skill" ([Eclipse review](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VYNmn4Tp1Jo); [YouTube search](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=%22An+Average+Campaign%22+roblox&sp=CAM%253D)). Fans pushed back hard, and the top replies defend the D&D framing and the idea of learning across many runs. The secrets also feed a large guide ecosystem: minmax and unlock videos draw 127K–412K views each ([YouTube search](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=%22An+Average+Campaign%22+roblox&sp=CAM%253D)).

**Design call.** Keep AAC's structure and rebuild the parts its critics and its genre gaps point at. The table maps each AAC system to what this game should do with it.

| AAC system | What AAC does (sourced above) | Design call for this game |
|---|---|---|
| Lobby and party | Tavern NPCs, 50-player lobby, no private servers | Harbor tavern plus a docked-ship hub. Add a party board, a cross-server queue, and private crew servers. |
| Run shape | Two linear acts of 8 and 12 stages, plus hidden branch areas | Three "seas" of about 6 node steps plus a boss each, on a visible but fogged chart. Keep hidden branch islands. |
| Between fights | Vote on 1 of 3 starred encounter cards, or Scavenge, or Short Rest | Keep the card-and-vote screen, but show the cards as the reachable islands on the chart. |
| Offense | Fight / Items / Focus, Energy and cooldowns | Keep. Add Brace (defend), Parley, and optional timed hits. |
| Defense | Block and Dodge rolled per hit | Replace with a per-player soul box. Turn the old defensive stats into soul-box parameters. |
| Subclasses | Unlocked by trainers in specific areas, last the run | Island mentors (a sword hermit, a ship's surgeon) |
| Death | Full wipe ends the run. Revives cost gold or Robux. | Last Stand, then DOWN, then revive at port. Permadeath only for NPC crew. |
| Meta | Cealts, Boons, Races, Prestige, Game Modifiers | Keep. Pay more for going deeper, and add ship-hub upgrades and a bounty "heat" dial. |
| Stress | None | Add a Resolve meter (Darkest Dungeon-style). |
| Puzzles | None, only stat skill checks | Add puzzles in three tiers. Show the odds on skill checks. |

## Each pillar owns a different clock, which is how they fit into one loop

**Design call.** The four genre pillars stop competing once each one owns a different time scale. JRPG commands own the player's turn. The soul box owns the enemy's turn. Darkest Dungeon's attrition owns the time between fights. The Inscryption chart owns the route. Puzzles own the time between runs. The table below is the voyage loop that results. Every step is a recommendation, and the right column names the pillar it comes from.

| Step | What the crew does | Borrowed from |
|---|---|---|
| Harbor (lobby) | Pick fighting style, origin and Legacies. Form a crew. Work on persistent hub puzzles. | AAC tavern; Inscryption cabin; DD Hamlet |
| Provision | Choose voyage length, buy supplies, assign crew jobs | DD1 provisioning |
| Chart | Vote on the next island among the reachable nodes; spend scouting | Inscryption board; Slay the Spire rules; StS2 co-op vote; AAC cards |
| Sea lane | Resolve a hazard; the Weather & Supplies dial drains | DD2 roads; DD light meter |
| Island | Fight, event or skill check, mentor, puzzle ruin, market, or treasure | AAC encounters; puzzle design |
| Fight | Command phase, then crew actions, then the soul-box enemy phase, then resolution | AAC/JRPG + Undertale + DD |
| Port (end of each sea) | Rest, cook, cure, craft, bank currency, save a checkpoint | DD2 Inn; AAC rest and shops |
| Voyage end | Boss win or wipe, then the wanted-poster summary, then spending meta currency | Inscryption deathcards; AAC Cealts; DD2 candles |

### Commands stay turn-based while enemy turns become a skill test

Undertale's design shows how the defense phase should work. Toby Fox built the battle engine before anything else and designed "bite-sized bullet patterns" drawn from Touhou and WarioWare. Each enemy owns a few attacks, and in group fights the patterns **combine** instead of playing one after another. Fox says this removes the "slog" of watching each enemy act in older RPGs ([Game Developer deep dive](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/game-design-deep-dive-i-undertale-i-s-action-based-rpg-battles)). He kept projectiles fewer and larger so players who are not bullet-hell experts could cope, and he made bosses harder by adding new gimmicks rather than more bullets ([PC Gamer](https://pcgamer.com/the-making-of-undertale), search snippet). Undertale's SOUL modes each change what the player can do. Red moves freely. Blue adds platformer gravity. Green locks the soul in place and blocks with a shield that turns to face four directions. Yellow shoots back. Purple slides along rope-like lines ([undertale.wiki: SOUL mode](https://undertale.wiki/w/SOUL_mode)). Deltarune adds the party economy. One soul dodges for everyone, and each attack is tagged **Targeted, Random or All** to decide whose HP drops. DEFEND cuts damage taken by a third and grants **16% Tension Points immediately**, so two defenders can fund a third character's 32% heal in the same turn. Grazing bullets earns TP and **shortens the enemy's attack**. A character knocked out drops to **−50% HP, cannot be targeted, and regains 1/8 of max HP per turn** until back on their feet, and the game is lost only when everyone is down ([deltarune.wiki: Target](https://deltarune.wiki/w/Target); [TP](https://deltarune.wiki/w/TP); [DOWN](https://deltarune.wiki/w/DOWN)).

The rest of the "active JRPG" toolkit is built on single-press timing. Mario & Luigi gives each brother one button for both attacking and dodging, and in Superstar Saga a successful action command doubles damage ([Super Mario Wiki](https://www.mariowiki.com/Action_command)). In Sea of Stars, every attack except Ultimates can be timed for extra power, and timed blocks reduce damage. Charging enemy spells show **locks**: break some to weaken the spell, or all of them to cancel it. Its director stresses that breaking 3 of 4 locks still weakens the attack a lot ([Sea of Stars wiki](https://seaofstars.fandom.com/wiki/Combat); [RPG Site interview](https://rpgsite.net/interview/14807-developer-debrief-chatting-with-the-mind-behind-sea-of-stars)). In Octopath Traveler, enemies "Break" after enough hits to their weaknesses and lose their next action ([Twinfinite](https://twinfinite.net/guides/break-and-boost-system-octopath-traveler-2-explained/)). In Persona 5, knocking an enemy down earns an extra turn, which can be **Baton Passed** to an ally with a bonus ([Megami Tensei Wiki](https://megatenwiki.com/wiki/Baton_Pass)). One Piece Odyssey is the closest official template. It used a **Power/Speed/Technique** rock-paper-scissors triangle and a Tension resource, but reviewers found the combat too easy, with no difficulty options and a cluttered UI ([GGRecon](https://ggrecon.com/reviews/one-piece-odyssey-review)). That is exactly the gap a soul box fills.

No shipped game has online co-op with a real-time bullet-hell defense phase. A fan-made Deltarune mod gives up to three *local* players **their own colored heart in a shared box**. This adds pressure but builds TP faster, and the mod's guide recommends easy mode to make up for remote-play lag ([AllThings.How](https://allthings.how/deltarune-local-multiplayer-mod-how-to-play-co-op-with-friends/)). Sea of Stars added three-player local co-op in which each player controls their own character ([Playday](https://playday.one/2024/11/13/sea-of-stars-free-dawn-of-equinox-available-co-op-combat-2-0-and-more/)). On timing windows, Clair Obscur: Expedition 33 players report parry windows of about 0.15 s on normal and about 0.35 s on Story, and the tighter window draws complaints. These are unofficial forum figures ([Steam forum](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1903340/discussions/0/592895604607223362)).

**Design call: the round.** Each combat round runs five phases. The design aims to keep a four-player round under about a minute.

| Phase | What happens | Borrowed from |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Intent | Every enemy shows an intent icon. Signature moves show 2–4 typed locks and a charge counter. | AAC's Energy telegraph; Sea of Stars locks |
| 2. Command (all at once, about 20 s timer) | Each living player picks Fight/Skill, Items, Focus (Energy), Brace (defend, which grants Tension immediately) or Parley. A player who times out auto-Braces. | AAC menu; Deltarune DEFEND |
| 3. Crew actions | Actions resolve in Initiative order. Each attacker gets an optional timed-hit press on their own screen, and hits on locks reduce the charge. | Sea of Stars; Deltarune FIGHT bar; Octopath Break |
| 4. Enemy phase (5–10 s; bosses up to 15 s) | All enemies' patterns combine into one seeded attack, and every player dodges in their own soul box. | Undertale combined patterns; Deltarune targeting |
| 5. Resolve | The server replays the dodge traces and applies damage, Resolve (stress) and Last Stand checks. | Darkest Dungeon |

**Design call: one box per player, with Deltarune's targeting rule.** Give every player their own box running the same seeded pattern, and use Deltarune's tags to decide who is in danger. A Targeted attack makes only the target's soul vulnerable, Random picks one or two players, and All threatens everyone. Each hit damages only the player who took it. This rules out "my friend's mistake killed me." It also keeps pattern density constant whatever the party size. To scale difficulty, raise enemy HP (AAC already adds 50% enemy HP per extra player ([an-average-campaign.pages.dev](https://an-average-campaign.pages.dev/))) and the number of Targeted attacks. It also lets each player choose their own assist settings. Players who are not targeted should still have something to do. Their soul becomes a "ghost" that earns Tension at a reduced rate by grazing, or they get one timed "Cover" press that shields the target. Routine attacks from weak enemies should resolve as a single Mario-style timed block instead of a full box. Save the box for signature moves, elites and bosses, so that rounds with four humans stay short.

**Design call: the old defensive stats become box parameters.** AAC's defensive stats should survive as soul-box modifiers. That way a player's build still matters alongside their reflexes, and a DEX-heavy rogue still "dodges more."

| AAC stat | Its AAC defensive role ([source](https://an-average-campaign.pages.dev/)) | Design call: its soul-box role |
|---|---|---|
| STR | +0.375% Block chance per point | Bigger damage reduction on a timed Guard or Cover |
| DEX | +0.175% Dodge per point (Dodge caps at 80%) | Faster soul and a slightly smaller hitbox, both capped |
| CON | +1 max HP and +0.375% Block damage reduction per point | HP and a flat damage reduction on each hit |
| LCK | Crit chance and crit damage | Wider graze radius; crits on timed hits |
| INT / FTH | +0.2% Energy gain per point | Faster Tension gain from grazing |
| CHA | Scales Bard support | Strength of Cover and cheer effects |

Keep AAC-style Block and Dodge rolls only for enemies and AI crew, where nobody is holding a controller, and show the result of every roll on screen.

**Design call: Tension, grazing and crew combos.** Run a crew-wide Tension pool like Deltarune's TP. Brace and grazing add to it immediately, so a support player can fund a teammate's big move in the same round. Grazing also shortens the enemy phase, but by the *party average*, so one expert player cannot skip the phase for everyone. Spend Tension on crew combination attacks (this game's version of Persona's Baton Pass), limited to one pass per player per round with a short timer, and on specials that break locks.

**Design call: escalate with new verbs, not more bullets.** Follow Fox's difficulty ladder. The first sea uses only the free-moving red mode with slow lanes. Mid-voyage fights combine two or three enemies' patterns. Each act boss brings a new soul mode: purple "lines" for a string-puppeteer boss, blue gravity for a thunder god on a sky island, green shield for a sniper firing from four sides, and yellow shooter for a counterattack phase against a cyborg. Build a library of reusable pattern pieces with adjustable settings (lanes, rings, sweeping lasers, homing shots, gravity jumps) and give each enemy two or three themed variations. Fox himself calls Deltarune's approach of building every element from scratch "inefficient and laborious" ([GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/games/rpg/toby-fox-says-the-smart-way-to-make-games-is-reusing-what-you-can-but-the-way-he-makes-deltarune-is-stupid-everything-in-the-game-is-simply-a-container-for-a-never-before-seen-gimmick); [Game Informer](https://gameinformer.com/2026/05/25/inefficient-and-laborious-the-ongoing-path-to-making-deltarune)).

**Design call: fewer, clearer damage types.** Collapse AAC's **16 damage types** ([Miraheze Damage Modifiers](https://anaveragecampaign.miraheze.org/wiki/Damage_Modifiers)) into Odyssey's Power/Speed/Technique triangle plus a few elements. Players have to check matchups inside a 20-second command window, and sixteen types cannot be read at a glance. Give enemies made of an element an "intangible" shield that only Armament-style Haki or a matching element can break. Breaking it works like Octopath: the enemy loses its next action and takes bonus damage.

**Design call: per-player assists.** Offer opt-in toggles that each player sets for themselves: a wider safe margin, slower bullets in your own box, and auto-timed basic hits at half the bonus. Sea of Stars ships equivalent toggles as Relics ([Prima Games](https://primagames.com/gaming/should-i-use-the-amulet-of-storytelling-and-sequent-flare-in-sea-of-stars); [Sea of Stars wiki: Difficulty Presets](https://seaofstars.fandom.com/wiki/Difficulty_Presets)). Make timed windows at least 150–200 ms by default. Add a hold-to-slow precision button; Deltarune halves soul speed while CANCEL is held ([deltarune.wiki: Target](https://deltarune.wiki/w/Target)). Flash a clear confirmation on every success.

### Darkest Dungeon's dread works only if afflictions change numbers, not control

Darkest Dungeon gives each hero a second health bar. When stress reaches **100**, the hero rolls a **25% chance of a Virtue and a 75% chance of an Affliction**. At **200**, a heart attack sends them straight to Death's Door ([darkestdungeon.wiki.gg: Virtue](https://darkestdungeon.wiki.gg/wiki/Virtue); [Death's Door](https://darkestdungeon.wiki.gg/wiki/Death%27s_Door)). At 0 HP a hero enters Death's Door. Each further hit then rolls a deathblow check against a **67% base resistance**, so there is a 33% chance of death per hit, and the resistance caps at 87%. Surviving leaves a "Death's Door Recovery" debuff that only certain camping skills remove ([Death's Door](https://darkestdungeon.wiki.gg/wiki/Death%27s_Door)). The light meter works as a risk dial the player controls. At Dark (1–25 light), loot gains +75%, stress gains +30%, and monsters get +10 accuracy and +10% damage. At Radiant, heroes get +7.5 dodge and are more likely to surprise monsters ([Light Meter](https://darkestdungeon.wiki.gg/wiki/Light_Meter)). Positioning is a puzzle of its own. Skills only work from certain ranks, so a back-line Arbalest shuffled to the front loses most of her kit, and the Shuffling Horror boss exists largely to scramble the party's order ([Shuffling Horror](https://darkestdungeon.wiki.gg/wiki/Shuffling_Horror)). Expeditions come in short, medium and long lengths with 0, 1 or 2 camps. Players buy provisions before leaving. A total party wipe loses the loot and sends the equipped trinkets to "The Shrieker," which players can later recover ([Expedition](https://darkestdungeon.wiki.gg/wiki/Expedition)).

**Design call: ranks.** Stage the 3D arena as AAC already does, with the crew on the left and enemies on the right, but give each side four ranks. Swordsmen and brawlers fight from the front, and snipers and navigators from the back. Skills only work from certain ranks, and knockback or pull skills drag enemies out of the ranks their best moves need. A position swap costs the turn of the player who starts it, so two humans never fight over a slot. Odyssey's Scramble Area Battle already split One Piece fights into zones where some skills reach only nearby enemies ([MP1st](https://mp1st.com/news/one-piece-odyssey-review-a-swashbuckling-adventure)). Reviewers found those zones added little strategy ([TechRaptor](https://Techraptor.net/gaming/reviews/one-piece-odyssey-review), search snippet), and hard rank-gating plus displacement is the fix.

**Design call: a Resolve meter.** Add a per-character **Resolve** bar (0–200) under HP. It rises when you take soul-box hits, get crit, watch a crewmate go down, sail through storms, or run low on supplies. It falls when someone cooks at a port, from musician skills and feasts, and from clean, no-hit dodges. At 100, roll Virtue against Affliction, but set the Virtue chance well above Darkest Dungeon's 25%. About 40% is a reasonable start, because the roll lands on a real player, not a disposable hero. An Affliction should change numbers and dialogue lines, never take over commands. "Panicked" might give +15% soul speed and a 10% bigger hitbox, and "Reckless" might add damage but disable Brace. Virtues are the shonen payoff moment.

**Design call: Last Stand.** Merge Death's Door with Deltarune's DOWN state. At 0 HP a character enters Last Stand: they can still act, with Darkest Dungeon-style penalties, and each further hit rolls a visible deathblow resistance that starts at 67%. Failing the roll knocks them DOWN (untargetable and regenerating each round) rather than out of the run. It also leaves a run-long "Scar" that only a port doctor or a specific rest action clears. The run still ends only on a full-crew wipe, as in AAC, and true permadeath applies only to recruited NPC crew. DD1-style permadeath of a player's main character would likely drive players away on a platform where many sessions are short.

**Design call: a Weather & Supplies dial, provisions, and bonds.** Turn the light meter into a ship-wide **Weather & Supplies** dial that drains with each sea lane. The crew, or whoever holds the Navigator job, can deliberately sail into worse weather for more loot and crits, at the cost of Resolve and tougher enemies. Before sailing, the crew picks a short, standard or long voyage and buys provisions: food against starvation debuffs, fuel for a ship special, bandages, and a scouting compass. Darkest Dungeon 2's affinity system lets pairs of heroes form relationships at each Inn stop, and those can sour ([Relationships](https://darkestdungeon.wiki.gg/wiki/Relationships)). Make bonds positive-only instead: players who fight together unlock combo attacks, so friends are never penalized for playing together.

### The sea chart replaces AAC's card screen without losing its vote

Inscryption's first act is a Slay the Spire-style branching map of four randomly generated maps. Nodes offer battles, card changes or items, the first three maps end in bosses, and the fourth ends with Leshy ([Wikipedia: Inscryption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inscryption)). The whole map is presented as a board game on Leshy's table. Lives are candles: losing twice on a map, or once to a boss, ends the run and turns the player into a "death card" that can appear in later runs ([Wikipedia: Inscryption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inscryption)). Its later acts swap the node map for an overworld similar to Pokémon and then for a room grid similar to The Binding of Isaac, with checkpoints, all built on the same card core ([Wikipedia: Inscryption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inscryption)). Slay the Spire builds each act on a 7×15 grid. Paths are visible from the start and never cross. It also enforces placement rules: no Elite or Rest Site below floor 6; no Elite, Merchant or Rest Site directly followed by another of the same type; and any room with two or more exits must lead to rooms of different types ([Kosgames](https://kosgames.com/slay-the-spire-map-generation-guide-26769/)). Slay the Spire 2's co-op mode, for up to 4 players, settles each fork with **a roulette weighted by votes**. Players can draw on the map, and the markings persist for the act. The path is shared, but decks, gold, rewards and shop stock are individual. A player at 0 HP sits out the fight and returns at 1 HP ([AllThings.How: StS2 co-op](https://allthings.how/slay-the-spire-2-co-op-multiplayer-every-mechanic-and-rule-explained/)). Darkest Dungeon 2 puts hazards on the roads between nodes. Hazardous and Rough roads cost Armor or a Wheel unless the stagecoach has the matching item. It also hides nodes in fog, and visiting a Watchtower reveals the rest of the region ([darkestdungeon.wiki.gg: Roads](https://darkestdungeon.wiki.gg/wiki/Roads)). On session length, Roblox's median session fell **from 36 to 26 minutes between 2023 and 2025**, while core users average 37 minutes ([GameAnalytics](https://www.gameanalytics.com/reports/2025-roblox-report), search summary).

**Design call: the voyage structure.** A full voyage has three seas plus optional hidden ones.

| Sea (act) | One Piece analog | Map shell | Length | Boss tier |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The four Blues | Open branching chart; two rows visible ahead | About 6 node steps + boss | Regional pirate captain |
| 2 | Paradise | Compass chain: mostly linear with a few forks. Each island needs a "charge" stay that also serves as a rest, shop or event. | About 6 + boss | Government-sanctioned warlord |
| 3 | New World | Three-needle compass: three choices every step; a shaking needle flags danger and better loot | About 6 + boss | Emperor-class pirate or navy admiral |
| Hidden | Sky island, undersea city | A bonus mini-act unlocked by a rare event, like AAC's Elven Camp leading to the Mines | 3–5 + boss | Unique |

**Design call: chart rules.** Present the map as a hand-drawn chart spread on the ship's navigation table, with a ship figurine that sails between island tokens. This is Leshy's board, recast. Lay out each sea as about 7 rows by 5 columns and apply Slay the Spire's rules. Row 1 is always a fight, a treasure island sits mid-sea, and a port comes just before the boss. There are no elites or rests in the first ~40% of the sea, no elite, shop or rest directly followed by another of the same kind, and every fork leads to different node types. Node types are: Navy patrol (fight), Bounty Target (an elite with its bounty shown), Treasure, Event "?" (an AAC-style narrated skill check), Mentor (subclass trainer), Ruins (puzzle), Market and Port. The edges are sea lanes that carry DD2-style hazards: a storm (hull damage), a sea monster (elite ambush), the doldrums (supplies lost) or a whirlpool. A matching ship upgrade or item cancels each hazard. Varying the map shell from sea to sea, as Inscryption varies it from act to act, keeps three acts from feeling identical.

**Design call: fog, compass and voting.** Show only the next one or two rows. The Navigator job, a crow's-nest upgrade, or a consumable "fixed compass" can reveal more rows or lock in a destination. When the crew has to choose, the reachable islands pop up as AAC's three-card vote screen. The chart adds long-range planning without throwing away AAC's proven interface. Resolve votes the StS2 way, with a 10–15 s timer, a roulette weighted by votes, and pings and drawing on the map. Never require a unanimous vote, and count AFK players as abstaining.

**Design call: ports as checkpoints and target length.** Each port banks currency and saves a checkpoint, so a crew that has to leave at minute 20 keeps about two-thirds of its earnings and can resume later. Aim for 30–40 minutes for a full three-sea voyage, and offer a one-sea "short voyage" of about 12 minutes. With AAC's average playtime at 26 minutes and Roblox's median session at 26 minutes, a full clear has to survive interruptions.

### Meta-progression should pay out on failed voyages and pay more for going deeper

Darkest Dungeon 2 pays its meta currency, Candles of Hope, in a steep curve: **2 for the tutorial region, 8 for the first region, 20 for the second, 36 for the third**, plus Mountain rewards. Even a failed early run earns something, while pushing deeper earns far more ([darkestdungeon.wiki.gg: Candles of Hope](https://darkestdungeon.wiki.gg/wiki/Candles_of_Hope)). Its Altar of Hope hub spends candles at locations that each mirror a region of the world. Its "Dam" location lets players toggle run modifiers, such as enabling retreat at a cost ([Altar of Hope](https://darkestdungeon.wiki.gg/wiki/Altar_of_Hope)). Inscryption turns failure into content. After a lost run, the player builds a deathcard from three cards in their deck, taking the cost from one, the stats from another and the sigils from the third, and names it. The card can reappear in later runs ([Inscryption Wiki: Deathcard](https://inscryption.fandom.com/wiki/Deathcard), search summary).

**Design call: currency and what it buys.** Use one meta currency (rename it; "Notoriety" works as a placeholder) and pay it out on a steep curve: roughly 5 for the first sea, 15 for the second, 30 for the third, plus a boss bonus. A full clear should land near AAC's ~70 Cealts. Spend it on four things. **Legacies** are AAC's 5-slot Boons, unlocked by achievements as in AAC. **Origins** are AAC's races, as original peoples such as merfolk, giantkin, beastfolk and skyfolk. **Prestige** works per fighting style. **Ship hub upgrades**, Darkest Dungeon's Hamlet recast, include a galley (Resolve relief), an infirmary (clears Scars), a workshop (crafting recipes), a crow's nest (scouting) and a recruitment board (NPC crew). Make AAC's Game Modifiers and DD2's Dam into a **Bounty Heat** dial: each notch makes the voyage harder and raises the currency multiplier. Keep each run's gear disposable, as AAC does, so every voyage gets fresh itemization.

**Design call: the wanted poster.** When the crew wipes, run Inscryption's deathcard ritual as a group. One player picks the poster's portrait, one picks its stat line and one picks its signature move, and the crew names the bounty. The poster then shows up in later voyages as a rival captain to hunt. It turns a loss into a shared creative moment, and its three picks split naturally across a 3–4 player crew. Use the same original poster layout for the end-of-run score screen.

### Complicated puzzles belong in the ship between runs, not inside them

Inscryption puts escape-room puzzles in the cabin between battles, and their progress survives a failed run. Daniel Mullins said the cabin puzzles "were a way of facilitating this persistent progression" in a roguelike ([Game Developer: Inscryption](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/how-game-jam-sacrifices-became-inscryption)). Clues are gated by run progress. The cabin safe's code appears in the rulebook only after a specific card has been seen in play ([GameSpot](https://www.gamespot.com/articles/inscryption-cabin-safe-guide-where-to-find-the-safe-combination/1100-6497509/), search summary). The Case of the Golden Idol was designed one self-contained puzzle and chapter at a time ([Thinky Games](https://thinkygames.com/features/how-the-case-of-the-golden-idol-developers-made-one-of-the-decades-best-detective-games-twice/), search summary). Tunic hides secrets in a manual written partly in an unreadable glyph language ([Game Developer: Tunic](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/road-to-igf-2023/how-tunic-weaves-wondrous-unknowable-worlds-inspired-by-inscrutable-nes-manuals)). Undertale's puzzles are mostly jokes that express Papyrus's character rather than real roadblocks, and critics call its areas "very linear, straightforward" ([Medium critical play](https://medium.com/game-design-fundamentals/critical-play-puzzles-in-undertale-5c27b9e6dcc8); [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/the-hidden-level-design-of-undertale)). For co-op, the We Were Here developers chose asymmetric information over a shared view. Separated players can talk only by walkie-talkie, and the developers prototyped that communication loop before writing any story ([Premortem.games](https://premortem.games/2022/05/31/total-mayhem-games-are-masters-of-the-asymmetrical-puzzler/)). AAC already gates some choices by class, such as "Pick the lock to the cell (Rogue)" ([Miraheze NPCs](https://anaveragecampaign.miraheze.org/wiki/NPCs)), and Odyssey gave each character a unique way of getting around the world ([GGRecon](https://ggrecon.com/reviews/one-piece-odyssey-review)).

**Design call: three tiers of puzzle.** Put puzzles in three tiers so the "complicated" ones never stall a run. **Tier 1, in-run micro-puzzles (30–90 s):** lever orders, current mazes, or a pompous rival's gag trap on Event nodes. These always have a skip or brute-force option that costs HP, supplies or Resolve. **Tier 2, island ruins (3–6 min):** an optional, self-contained puzzle room in the Golden Idol style, placed on Ruins, Treasure or elite islands, that pays out in relics. **Tier 3, the persistent meta puzzle:** an ancient-stone script (an original stand-in for Poneglyphs) whose fragments drop during voyages and are decoded in the ship hub between runs. Each decoded line unlocks lore, starting items or a secret final route. This is where real difficulty belongs, because failing a voyage never erases it. Gate clues behind reaching specific islands, as Inscryption gates its safe code.

**Design call: asymmetric co-op and anti-spoiler rules.** Make co-op puzzles asymmetric, using Roblox's per-player GUI. One player sees the key (a codebook page, a glyph table) on their own screen while another stands at the mechanism, and the in-world snail-phone is the walkie-talkie. Give each crew job a "lens": the Navigator sees currents, the Archaeologist reads glyphs, and the Lookout spots distant switches. That way no clever player solves everything while the others watch. Randomize codes and symbol orders per crew and per run, because a single fixed solution will be on YouTube within a day in a guide-heavy community like AAC's. Keep AAC's stat skill checks, but **show the odds**. Eclipse's complaint about opaque outcomes is fair, while the hidden branches and secret unlocks that feed AAC's guide scene can stay secret.

### Crew play has to beat the waiting problem of four humans in one fight

Every pillar adds waiting time, and every extra player multiplies it. The research found no professional postmortem on an online multiplayer turn-based bullet-hell hybrid, so these rules come from adjacent precedents. What is documented: AAC was criticized for matchmaking friction and does not allow private servers ([Eclipse review](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VYNmn4Tp1Jo); [Roblox Games API](https://games.roblox.com/v1/games?universeIds=7359962123)). Slay the Spire 2 settles duplicate relic claims with an automatic rock-paper-scissors and lets a "Mend" rest heal a teammate ([AllThings.How](https://allthings.how/slay-the-spire-2-co-op-multiplayer-every-mechanic-and-rule-explained/)). The Straw Hats' canonical jobs (captain, swordsman, navigator, sniper, cook, doctor, archaeologist, shipwright, musician, helmsman) were each recruited in their own island arc ([One Piece Wiki: Straw Hat Pirates](https://onepiece.fandom.com/wiki/Straw_Hat_Pirates); canon reference, not re-fetched).

**Design call: crew rules.** Support 1–5 players and tune for 3–4. Take all commands at the same time with a visible timer. Combine enemy patterns into one phase. Run every player's soul box in parallel. Turn players who time out repeatedly into AI pilots, and let the crew vote to kick them. Share the path and encounters, but give each player their own rewards and shop stock, as StS2 does. A shared stash, as in AAC, lets gold and items move between players. A quick janken (rock-paper-scissors) settles disputes over unique items, which is also an on-theme gag. Add a light second axis besides fighting style: a **crew job** picked when the voyage starts, unique within the crew, that grants a map or camp ability rather than combat skills. The Navigator scouts, the Cook feeds the Resolve rest, the Doctor clears Scars, the Shipwright repairs the hull, and the Musician shields against stress. With fewer than four players, recruited NPC crew fill the missing jobs at reduced strength. This makes solo play viable and gives the Parley command a purpose: parleying with a beaten enemy can recruit them. Fix AAC's matchmaking complaint directly with a party board in the harbor, a cross-server queue, private crew servers, and the option to resume a voyage from the last port.

## One Piece's geography and powers already work as game systems

Lore details beyond Wikipedia's summary come from the One Piece Wiki and were not re-fetched during research, so spot-check them before writing lore text. One Piece's world is mostly ocean, divided by two great lines. The **Red Line**, a mountain range that spans the world, runs along the Prime Meridian. The **Grand Line** runs along the equator, splitting the sea into **North, East, West and South Blue**. The Grand Line is walled in by the **Calm Belt**, windless water full of ship-eating **Sea Kings**, and the Red Line splits it into **"Paradise"** and the **"New World"** ([Wikipedia: One Piece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Piece)). Inside the Grand Line, ordinary compasses fail. A **Log Pose** locks onto the next island after a stay of varying length, an **Eternal Pose** points permanently at one island, and the New World Log Pose has three needles, where a shaking needle marks danger ([One Piece Wiki: Log Pose](https://onepiece.fandom.com/wiki/Log_Pose); [Eternal Pose](https://onepiece.fandom.com/wiki/Eternal_Pose)). Devil Fruits give transforming powers but take away the ability to swim, and Haki is a willpower-based ability that is one of the few ways to hurt certain fruit users ([Wikipedia: One Piece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Piece)). Canon groups fruits into Paramecia, Zoan and Logia types. Logia users are untouchable unless the attacker uses Haki, and seastone drains users like sea water does ([One Piece Wiki: Devil Fruit](https://onepiece.fandom.com/wiki/Devil_Fruit)). Haki comes in Observation, Armament and Conqueror's forms ([One Piece Wiki: Haki](https://onepiece.fandom.com/wiki/Haki)). The government's "Six Powers" martial art includes a flash-step, an air-walk, an iron body, a finger strike, a kicked air blade and a paper-like evasion ([One Piece Wiki: Rokushiki](https://onepiece.fandom.com/wiki/Rokushiki)). The power structure has a World Government with a navy and a secret police, a Revolutionary Army, and pirate tiers of "Four Emperors" and "Seven Warlords" ([Wikipedia: One Piece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Piece)). Bounties reflect perceived threat, not just strength ([One Piece Wiki: Bounties](https://onepiece.fandom.com/wiki/Bounties)).

**Design call: map each canon concept to a system and an original name.** The names below are placeholders. They show the level of renaming that keeps the mechanic while dropping the coined term.

| Canon concept | System it becomes | Original placeholder name |
|---|---|---|
| Grand Line and Log Pose chain | Sea 2 compass-chain map shell; the island "charge" stay becomes a rest/shop node | "the Meridian," "lodestone compass" |
| Eternal Pose | Consumable that reveals or locks a destination | "fixed lodestone" |
| Calm Belt and Sea Kings | Hazard sea lanes; elite sea monsters with large bullet patterns (tidal walls, coils) | "the Doldrums," "deep tyrants" |
| Devil Fruits (Paramecia / Zoan / Logia) | Power overlay that lasts one run (one per character, can't swim) on trickster / bruiser-stance / intangible-elemental archetypes | "cursed fruits," "Flame Fruit" |
| Seastone; sea water | A Silence status that blocks fruit skills; a Soaked status that drains Energy | "sea-iron" |
| Observation / Armament / Conqueror's Haki | Dodge aids (preview telegraphs, wider graze, brief slow) / damage reduction and intangibility bypass / late-game fear and enemy-stress damage | "Will": Sense, Steel, Crown |
| Six Powers martial art | Pattern set for government agents: teleport, aerial bullet sources, crescent waves, point shots, invulnerable phases, an evader that forces AoE | An original six-move art |
| Bounties; Marines, Cipher Pol, Revolutionaries, Warlords, Emperors | Heat stat, score screen and wanted-poster deathcards; faction node types and boss tiers | "Notoriety"; original faction names |
| Crew roles | Crew jobs (generic words such as Navigator and Cook are safe) | Navigator, Cook, Doctor, Shipwright, Musician, Lookout |
| Poneglyphs; Den Den Mushi | Tier-3 meta puzzle script; narrator and asymmetric-puzzle channel | "waystones"; a "shell-phone" snail |

**Design call: classes.** Reskin AAC's seven classes as fighting styles. Warrior becomes **Swordsman** (dual-blade, draw-strike or greatsword mentors). Brawler becomes **Brawler** (fists or a kick-only style). Ranger becomes **Sniper** (trick ammunition). Mage becomes **Stormcaller** (a weather staff). Priest becomes **Doctor**. Bard becomes **Musician**. Rogue becomes **Cat Burglar**. Each subclass is unlocked by an island mentor, exactly as AAC's trainers are. A cursed fruit sits on top of any style as a commitment for that run, one per character as in canon. It adds fruit skills and a big weakness: sea hazards and Soaked statuses hurt fruit users, so crews want at least one swimmer. Fruit types line up with AAC's existing roles: Zoan as a tank stance, Logia as an evasive elementalist that takes no damage until an enemy shows Steel, and Paramecia as a utility trickster.

**Design call: biomes and bosses.** Build original islands on canon's templates: a winter kingdom with a doctor mentor, a desert kingdom in civil war, a shipbuilding canal city, a ghost ship, a split fire-and-ice island, and hidden sky and undersea mini-acts reached through a sky-launching geyser or a deep-sea event. Factions become node types, such as navy patrols, secret-agent ambushes, revolutionary contacts and bounty hunters. Bosses climb from regional captain to sanctioned warlord to emperor or admiral. Raising Bounty Heat should make admiral-class "hunter" nodes appear more often. That turns notoriety into a visible threat, the way canon bounties track threat rather than strength.

The market gap is real. **Blox Fruits** drew about 378K–407K concurrent players in early November 2026 and hit an all-time high of **929,758 on October 31, 2026** ([LiveCounts](https://livecounts.nl/roblox-game-player-count/?id=2753915549)). **Sailor Piece** has posted snapshots of 344,739 and 402,558, an outcome credited to Blox Fruits fatigue and power creep ([RoWatcher](https://rowatcher.com/news/king-legacy-vs-blox-fruits-vs-sailor-piece-ranking-every-one-piece-roblox-rpg)). King Legacy sits around 35–37K and GPO around 10.8K ([RoWatcher](https://rowatcher.com/news/king-legacy-vs-blox-fruits-vs-sailor-piece-ranking-every-one-piece-roblox-rpg); [myleafs](https://www.myleafs.com/app/roblox/game-worth/1730877806)). A September 2026 top-10 list describes no turn-based, roguelite or bullet-hell mechanics in any of them ([Mobi.gg](https://mobi.gg/en/tops/top-best-one-piece-games-on-roblox/)). Licensed games have underperformed. Bandai Namco's official **One Piece Grand Arena** peaked at about **3,431 concurrent players** ([Rolimons](https://www.rolimons.com/game/18139069423)). The game will not reach Blox Fruits numbers, because it targets players who want a crew campaign, not a level-2,550 grind. But AAC shows that this format can hold thousands of concurrent players at 94% approval. Mainstream attention is also rising: Netflix's live-action Season 2 released on March 10, 2026, and Wit Studio's remake is slated for 2027 ([Wikipedia: One Piece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Piece)).

## A brighter AAC: blocky crew, pixel soul box, nautical tabletop UI

AAC looks deliberately cheap in-game and expensive in marketing. Researchers inspected frames from a full-run video of the late-2026 build. The interface is **flat, dark-gray translucent panels in a chunky pixel font** over a live 3D scene. A "Turn N" header and an initiative list with thin red HP and blue Energy bars sit top-left, with an inspector panel for the selected unit below them. The player's own plate sits bottom-center, showing class, level, XP, HP, Energy and gold. Status icons with tooltips sit bottom-right, and a semi-transparent banner across mid-screen names every ability as it is used. An "area (n/N)" progress counter sits top-right ([Wetlag video ~27:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=e83JrLu2YZA&t=1620s)). Party members stand on the left of a 3D arena and enemies on the right, under a three-quarter camera, with small BillboardGui plates showing each combatant's name and bars ([Wetlag video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=e83JrLu2YZA&t=1620s)). The route screen, titled "Where To Next?", shows three tall cards in muted slate-blue, maroon and sage. Each card has a 3D preview render, a one-line second-person flavor sentence, a live vote count and difficulty stars, with Scavenge, a countdown timer and Short Rest underneath ([Wetlag video ~25:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=e83JrLu2YZA&t=1500s); [route-select thumbnail](https://tr.rbxcdn.com/180DAY-f5e7ca4def451e00ffbb3035fa6dc2ed/768/432/Image/Webp/noFilter)). Narrated events fill most of the screen: a square illustration on the left, then a title, earnest second-person narration, and choice buttons showing live votes. Camp menus end with a party-wide "Ready (x/4)" button ([Wetlag video ~28:20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=e83JrLu2YZA&t=1700s); [~0:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=e83JrLu2YZA&t=40s)). The game world is low-poly, moodily lit environments with forced R6 avatars. The key art, by contrast, is **painted anime and ink-wash dark fantasy**: a monochrome Bone Drake looming over blocky party silhouettes, a red-and-black knight, and a cozy campfire of Roblox avatars ([Bone Drake key art](https://tr.rbxcdn.com/180DAY-9544a65b006601e9d6486e8f6bfdbbc6/768/432/Image/Webp/noFilter); [campfire key art](https://tr.rbxcdn.com/180DAY-f0b0808c6e364f3db4ea0237be87f84d/768/432/Image/Webp/noFilter)). The tone pairs sincere, grim narration with meme names, such as an "Aura Farmer" NPC and a Boon called "Ourpled." There is no Dungeon Master character, and the music comes from library tracks with no composer credit ([Miraheze NPCs](https://anaveragecampaign.miraheze.org/wiki/NPCs); [Roblox game page](https://www.roblox.com/games/80734098185936/An-Average-Campaign)). Researchers could not confirm floating damage numbers or miss, block and dodge pop-ups in the sampled frames.

**Design call: the vibe.** Aim for *sincere high-seas adventure, slapstick naming, and found family under pressure*. Keep AAC's split between earnest narration and joke names, but brighten the mood. One Piece swings between feasts and slapstick and grim arcs, so frame Darkest Dungeon's stress as "the crew's bonds hold them together" rather than as grimdark. Add the narrator AAC lacks. An in-world chronicler voice, whether a snail-phone broadcaster or a news bird delivering events, can comment on votes and wipes the way Leshy comments on Inscryption's board. That makes cheap content feel personal. The self-mocking "An Average ___" naming tradition is part of the brand's charm. YouTube commenters already treat it as a genre ([Eclipse review comments](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VYNmn4Tp1Jo)).

**Design call: art direction.** Copy AAC's cost structure, not its palette. Keep forced R6 avatars and low-poly islands, but light them in bright, saturated daylight with exaggerated silhouettes, so the dread comes from the systems rather than the lighting. Make the soul box a deliberate contrast layer: crisp pixel art in an Undertale register, a square box dropped over a dimmed 3D arena while the 3D enemy plays its wind-up animation. Commission original anime-style key art of an *original* crew for thumbnails, as AAC does for its dark fantasy, and never draw canon characters.

**Design call: UI.** The table maps each screen to its AAC reference and the recommended change.

| Screen | AAC reference (observed) | Design call |
|---|---|---|
| Battle HUD | Turn list, inspector, bottom player plate, status tooltips, mid-screen action banner, area counter | Keep the layout. Add a Resolve bar under HP, rank markers, enemy intent icons, and pop-ups for damage, Graze, Block and Miss. Reskin with parchment, rope and brass 9-slice frames. Use larger text for phones. |
| Enemy phase | Does not exist | A square soul box at center over the dimmed arena, a target reticle on the targeted players' portraits, the Tension meter beside the box, and a one-second countdown before bullets spawn |
| Route choice | "Where To Next?" cards with votes, stars and a timer | The chart on the navigation table with fog. Reachable islands rise as AAC-style cards with votes and a timer, plus pings and drawing. |
| Events | A panel with an illustration, second-person narration and voted choices | Keep it. Add a narrator portrait, and show success odds on stat checks. |
| Inventory and stash | Three columns with a D&D stat sheet and a "Ready (x/4)" button | Keep it, but split it into tabs on mobile. Show soul-box stats (speed, hitbox, graze) next to the old sheet. |
| Run end | Not documented | A wanted-poster summary with an original layout |

Readability rules also come from the sources. Inscryption's designer cut all card text except the name and used sigil icons instead, because the game rendered at low resolution ([Game Developer: Inscryption](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/how-game-jam-sacrifices-became-inscryption)). Sea of Stars ships a toggle that confirms every successful timed input with a visual flare ([Prima Games](https://primagames.com/gaming/should-i-use-the-amulet-of-storytelling-and-sequent-flare-in-sea-of-stars)). **Design call:** Use icons over text, give each projectile a telegraph of about 0.3–0.5 s before it can hurt, and keep a consistent color language: black-edged bullets can't be blocked, and element colors mark matchups. For audio, commission original sea shanty-flavored themes. Crossfade between the chart, battle and port themes, and time bullet patterns to the beat using server time.

**Design call: target a Mild rating.** Enemies faint or vanish at 0 HP, there is no blood, and the crew drinks juice and eats meat, never sake. Roblox counts unrealistic violence where bodies disappear at 0 health as Mild. Alcohol references must be disclosed, and Minimal and Mild experiences stay eligible for Roblox Kids (5–8) and Roblox Select (9–15), where much of the One Piece fan base sits ([Roblox docs: Content maturity](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/promotion/content-maturity); [Experience guidelines](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/promotion/experience-guidelines)).

## Roblox can carry this design with a GUI soul box and a server state machine

No mature, open-source Roblox "Undertale battle engine" exists. A late-2025 DevForum thread asking how to build one got only general advice, and the "Undertale Battle Engine" experience open-sources only its asset loader ([DevForum](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/how-would-i-go-about-making-an-undertale-style-battle-system/4028030); [Roblox: Undertale Battle Engine](https://www.roblox.com/games/18508929778/Undertale-Battle-Engine)). Community reports put the real cost in **per-frame property writes, not instance counts**. One developer handled about 180 client-side bullets per frame, another saw lag at about 150 3D-part bullets, and pooling is standard practice ([DevForum](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/handling-thousands-of-projectiles-with-high-performance/735531); [DevForum](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/more-performant-way-to-create-a-projectile/2312096); [DevForum](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/optimizing-manipulation-of-thousands-of-parts/2944309)). The best reply in a 2022 bullet-hell thread said the server should send "information about each attack," not per-bullet updates, so every client generates the same deterministic pattern ([DevForum](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/strategy-to-optimize-performance-on-a-bullet-hell-game-genre-in-roblox/1706889)). On the engine side, `RenderStepped` is superseded by `PreRender` ([RunService docs](https://create.roblox.com/docs/reference/engine/classes/RunService)). Since **September 24, 2026**, rotated GUI children are clipped correctly by a non-rotated `ClipsDescendants` parent ([DevForum](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/opt-in-phase-introducing-clipping-support-for-rotated-elements-path2d/4737864)). An `EditableImage` canvas tops out at 1024×1024, has to be enabled in Creator Dashboard settings, works within strict client memory budgets, and **only one updates on screen per frame** ([EditableImage docs](https://create.roblox.com/docs/reference/engine/classes/EditableImage)). `UnreliableRemoteEvent` drops payloads over 1,000 bytes and is capped at about 500 requests per second per client ([UnreliableRemoteEvent docs](https://create.roblox.com/docs/reference/engine/classes/UnreliableRemoteEvent)). Knit was archived on July 31, 2024, and Roact is deprecated in favor of react-lua ([GitHub: Knit](https://github.com/Sleitnick/Knit); [GitHub: Roact](https://github.com/Roblox/roact)). ProfileStore is the maintained successor to ProfileService, with session locking ([DevForum: ProfileStore](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/profilestore/3190543)). `ReserveServer` and `TeleportPartyAsync` are deprecated in favor of `TeleportAsync` with `TeleportOptions.ReservedServerId`, at most 50 players per call, and the docs warn that "Exploiters can spoof teleport data" ([TeleportService docs](https://create.roblox.com/docs/reference/engine/classes/TeleportService)). The Input Action System and its Studio editor are fully released ([Input Action System docs](https://create.roblox.com/docs/input/input-action-system); [DevForum: Input Action Manager](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/full-release-input-action-manager/4965021/1)). Uploading custom audio is capped at **10 tracks a month by default, or 100 with ID verification** ([DevForum: audio privacy](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/action-needed-upcoming-changes-to-asset-privacy-for-audio/1701697?page=3)).

**Design call: the technical stack.**

| Concern | Recommended approach |
|---|---|
| Places and sessions | A lobby place (harbor hub, up to 50 players) and a run place. Start a voyage with `TeleportAsync` using a fresh GUID as the `ReservedServerId`, and set the run place's max players to the party cap. Treat TeleportData as a hint only. |
| Battle logic | One server-side state machine per battle (Intent, Command, Crew actions, Enemy phase, Resolve, End check). Clients send only intents through a small, validated remote surface: SubmitAction, SubmitDodgeTrace and a State broadcast. |
| Soul box | Pooled ImageLabels inside a square frame with `ClipsDescendants` on, simulated in a fixed 240×240 virtual space with circle collision. Step it at a fixed 1/60 s inside `PreRender`, write only changed positions, and cap the box at about 150–300 bullets until you profile on a low-end Android phone. These counts are extrapolated, not benchmarked. |
| Alternative renderer | An `EditableImage` canvas (about 256×256, Pixelated) for a pure pixel look. Fall back to the GUI renderer if creation returns nil, and never run two changing canvases at once. |
| Sync and anti-cheat | The server sends `{attackId, seed, startTime, duration}`. Each client builds the pattern from a seeded RNG and streams a compact, quantized soul-position trace. The server replays the deterministic pattern and computes hits itself, enforcing a maximum soul speed and staying in bounds. Add a 1–2 s grace window after each phase and a 0.5–1 s countdown before it. |
| Ghost souls | Teammates' souls sent at 10–20 Hz over `UnreliableRemoteEvent` as quantized int16 pairs, well under the 1,000-byte cap |
| Frameworks | Plain ModuleScripts with your own service loader; react-lua, Fusion or Vide for UI; ProfileStore for saves; optionally Blink or Zap for typed buffers ([DevForum: Zap/ByteNet](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/zap-or-bytenet-performance-security/2888968)) |
| Saves and rejoin | Write a `currentRun` record (runId, seed, node index, HP, Resolve, inventory, version) at each node checkpoint, never mid-battle. Keep a runId-to-reserved-server mapping in MemoryStore so players who drop can rejoin ([Memory stores docs](https://create.roblox.com/docs/cloud-services/memory-stores)). |
| Map | Generate on the server from a seeded RNG and send the graph, not just the seed, to avoid version drift. Draw it in a ScrollingFrame with ImageButton nodes, and draw edges with rotated thin Frames or [Path2D](https://create.roblox.com/docs/ui/2D-paths) curves. Animate the ship along the chosen path. |
| Input and layout | The Input Action System for keyboard and gamepad (a Direction2D action for the soul). A custom touch "trackpad" drag on the lower half of the screen plus a slow button, so the finger never covers the soul. `UIAspectRatioConstraint` = 1 on the box, `ScreenInsets` for notches ([ScreenInsets docs](https://create.roblox.com/docs/reference/engine/enums/ScreenInsets)), `ResampleMode = Pixelated` and integer `UIScale` for pixel art ([ImageLabel docs](https://create.roblox.com/docs/reference/engine/classes/ImageLabel)). |
| Audio | Upload under the group that owns the experience. Budget for a theme per sea plus boss stings within the monthly caps, and never upload anime music. |
| Engine features to skip | Roblox's Server Authority mode is physics-oriented and forces engine-wide settings such as deferred signals ([Server authority docs](https://create.roblox.com/docs/projects/server-authority)). It adds risk and no benefit to a GUI minigame. |

**Design call: build order for the vertical slice.** Build in risk order. First, the server battle state machine with simultaneous commands and AFK auto-Brace, playable by four testers. Second, the soul box with three primitive patterns, seeded replay and the touch trackpad, profiled on a cheap phone. Third, one sea of the chart with voting and two node types. Fourth, the lobby, the reserved-server teleport and rejoin. Fifth, ProfileStore meta saves and the wanted poster. Only then build Resolve, ranks, puzzles and content. The four-player round timer is the single number most likely to make or break the game, so measure it first.

## No license exists, so the world must be original

Roblox's policy is blunt. When a valid DMCA notice arrives, Roblox "acts quickly" to take the content down. A counter-notice usually restores it in about 14 business days unless the claimant shows it has sued. Infringement can occur "even if you credited the creator, didn't profit, or believed fair use applied," and repeat infringers can lose their accounts ([Roblox Creator Docs: DMCA guidelines](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/publishing/dmca-guidelines)). Since 2024, **Rights Manager** has let verified rights holders file removal requests directly against experiences ([Roblox Creator Docs: Rights Manager](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/publishing/rights-manager)). **License Manager**, launched July 15, 2025, lets them register IP, **scan experiences for IP use**, and license it at fees Roblox expects to run 15–50% of revenue ([GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/roblox-launches-self-serve-licensing-enabling-creators-to-team-with-ip-brands/)). **Toei, Shueisha, Bandai Namco and One Piece do not appear in Roblox's licensing catalog** ([Roblox Creator Docs: IP licensing](https://create.roblox.com/docs/en-us/ip-licensing)). Toei's 2024 Roblox licensing partnership covered an original film, not One Piece ([Anime News Network](https://www.animenewsnetwork.com/press-release/2024-08-06/spaceport-announces-intellectual-property-licensing-partnership-with-toei-animation-to-license-/.213995)). Rights holders are on the platform anyway: Netflix's *East Blue Brawls* (2024) and Bandai Namco's *Grand Arena* are official One Piece Roblox games ([ONE Esports](https://www.oneesports.gg/gaming/one-piece-roblox-east-blue-brawls/); [Anime News Network](https://www.animenewsnetwork.com/news/2024-08-10/one-piece-grand-arena-game-announced-for-roblox/.214156)).

The track record of One Piece-inspired games is reassuring but not proof of safety. Researchers found **no verified DMCA takedown of a major One Piece-inspired Roblox game**. A 2022 claim that Blox Fruits was being taken down is unverified ([X post](https://x.com/RobloxNoobifier/status/1561561887829069825)), and the game still ranked #2 on Roblox in November 2026 ([LiveCounts](https://livecounts.nl/roblox-game-player-count/?id=2753915549)). A DevForum thread describes an unnamed game removed after a **Bandai Namco** notice ([DevForum](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/game-deleted-without-warning-or-reason/1064764)). The big incumbents avoid the exact mark in their titles (Blox Fruits, Grand Piece Online, King Legacy, Sailor Piece), though GPO still uses fruit names that echo the source, such as "Gomu Gomu" ([Mobi.gg](https://mobi.gg/en/tops/top-best-one-piece-games-on-roblox/); [Dot Esports](https://dotesports.com/roblox/news/grand-piece-online-gpo-tier-list-all-fruit-ranked)). The upside case is Blue Lock: Rivals. It was an unlicensed fan game that Kodansha brought under its licensing program instead of removing, and Tubefilter's view is that IP owners tolerate free fan games until they earn large sums ([Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2025/07/15/roblox-ip-platform-netflix-blue-lock-creator-revenue-share-transformative-works/)). Nothing indicates One Piece's rights holders plan to do the same.

The research notes rank the risk on a ladder, as an inference from general copyright and trademark principles. This is not legal advice.

| Risk level | What the game uses | Where the incumbents sit |
|---|---|---|
| 1 (lowest) | One Piece-like *mechanics* with original names: a power fruit that blocks swimming, "Will" instead of Haki, a lodestone compass | Mostly here |
| 2 | Distinctive coined terms: Haki, Log Pose, Devil Fruit, Sea King, Gomu Gomu, Rokushiki | Partly here |
| 3 | Canon characters and places: named crew members, canon islands | Generally avoided |
| 4 (highest) | Official art, logos, music, the "One Piece" title, or likenesses on wanted posters | Avoided |

**Design call: mitigation.** Treat IP safety as an architecture decision, not a marketing afterthought.

Rename the public title. "An Average One Piece" uses the exact mark in a way that could imply affiliation. An "An Average ___" title that keeps the meme without the mark (for example, "An Average Voyage") keeps the genre signal and drops the hook.

Stay at level 1. Use the original placeholder names from the setting table, invent original captains, islands and factions, and design an original Jolly Roger and an original wanted-poster layout.

Store every player-facing name in one localization-style data table. A forced rename then becomes a config change rather than a content rewrite.

Commission original music and never upload anime audio. Keep marketing art to your original crew.

Describe the game as a "pirate crew roguelite," not as a One Piece game.

Keep monetization in AAC's light register (Cealt-style bundles, a revive, no IP-forward paid cosmetics), since takedown attention appears to grow with revenue and visibility.

Publish under a group, and know the counter-notice process before you need it.

The same discipline applies to AAC itself. Borrow its systems and interface patterns, but none of its art, text or names.

## Conclusion

The bullet-hell layer is more than a feature bolted onto AAC. It repairs the exact weakness AAC's critics named. AAC's defense is the part of the game where players have the least agency, so replacing dice with a soul box whose speed, hitbox and graze radius still come from the stat sheet keeps AAC's build-craft and adds skill, without losing the tabletop identity fans defended. The same move turns Haki, the canon's most recognizable power system, into something players physically perform rather than read off a percentage. The real design risk is time, not genre fit. Every pillar adds waiting, four humans multiply it, and no shipped game has proven online co-op bullet-hell inside turn-based combat. The design therefore depends on simultaneous commands, combined enemy patterns, per-player boxes and a one-minute round budget, and that budget should be the first thing prototyped and measured.

The research also changes how the One Piece question should be framed. The setting's value lies in its *structures*: a compass that points one island ahead, a fruit that trades swimming for power, a bounty that grows with notoriety, a crew assembled one job at a time. All of these survive renaming intact. Its names, faces and logos are where nearly all the legal exposure sits. A game built that way can enter an empty niche next to 400K-CCU grinders as the one One Piece-flavored game about being a crew, and it can do so without the platform's growing removal and IP-scanning tools ever having a reason to look at it.
